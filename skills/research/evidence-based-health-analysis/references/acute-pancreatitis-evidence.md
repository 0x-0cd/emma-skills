## AP Aftermath — Disease Progression Cascade

```
First AP episode
    │
    ├─► 25% → Recurrent AP (RAP)
    │         │
    │         ├─► CP progression (especially alcoholic/smoking)
    │         └─► PPDM risk (RAP OR 25.3)
    │
    ├─► 6% → Chronic Pancreatitis (CP)
    │         ├─► PPDM (~25-40% within 5yr)
    │         ├─► PEI (exocrine insufficiency)
    │         └─► PC risk (through CP pathway)
    │
    ├─► ~25% → PPDM / Type 3c diabetes
    │         ├─► 70% need long-term insulin
    │         └─► Higher MACE/mortality than T2DM
    │
    └─► Cardiovascular risk (MACCE)
              └─► Men HR 1.80, MI HR 2.75 vs women
```

## Lifestyle Interventions — Effect Sizes

| Intervention | Effect | Evidence Level |
|-------------|--------|---------------|
| Smoking cessation | Reduces CP risk (RR 2.5→1.4 for ex-smokers); PC risk (OR 2.2→1.2). Takes ~20yr to normalize. | Strong (multiple cohorts + meta-analysis) |
| Alcohol abstinence | CP progression: 14% abstinent vs 41% continued heavy drinking. RCT: counseling reduces recurrence. | Strong (RCT + cohort) |
| Metformin in PPDM | Severe hypo -59%, MACE -26%, mortality -44% | Nationwide cohort |
| Mediterranean diet | Associated with lower PC risk | Moderate (cohort) |
| Weight loss | Reduces insulin resistance → lowers PPDM risk | Moderate (observational + mechanistic) |
| Strength training | Increases muscle mass → improves insulin sensitivity | Indirect/moderate |

## Common Missing Lab Items in Routine Checkups

Patients with AP history often miss these in standard physical exams:
- HbA1c (not in basic packages)
- Fasting insulin (rarely ordered)
- Fecal elastase-1 (very rarely ordered, needs explicit request)
- Lipid panel (often included but worth verifying)

## Key Communication Points

- AP recovery ≠ disease-free — it's a "sentinel event" for future metabolic/pancreatic/cardiovascular risk
- Absolute risk language: "About 1 in 100 AP patients develop pancreatic cancer within 5 years, vs ~0.4 in 100 in general population"
- Don't induce anxiety — focus on modifiable factors (weight, exercise, diet, smoking, alcohol)
- Lifestyle changes are the main intervention; medication rarely needed in early stages
- The younger the patient at first AP, the more years of risk accumulation ahead → more incentive to intervene early
